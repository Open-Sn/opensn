class MeshSegmentTracer

Exact traversal of straight segments through the local cells of a mesh.

Every cell is the union of its side tetrahedra, each formed by a side triangle of a face (two consecutive face vertices and the face centroid) and the cell centroid. This requires the cell centroid to lie on the inner side of every side triangle, as the piecewise-linear discretizations also do. The tracer rejects a cell that violates it. A cell for which all of its vertices and face centroids lie on the inner side of the plane of every side triangle is convex, and is the intersection of the half-spaces of its side triangles. Any other cell is traced through its side tetrahedra, which are convex.

A segment from start to end is parameterized by its distance t from start along its fixed direction. For each convex piece the tracer computes the exact interval of t in which the line lies in the piece, from the planes of the piece alone. It never moves the ray or uses a point computed in another cell. A cell continues the traversal at t when its interval begins at t, within a tolerance, and extends beyond it. The tolerance is 1e-10 of the cell size or segment length plus the round-off of the coordinates, so that it stays far below the cell size wherever the mesh lies. Whether a point of the line lies in a piece is decided from its distances to the planes, which round off like the coordinates, and not from the values of t at which the line crosses them, which lose accuracy for a plane that the line grazes. The next cell is looked for first among the neighbors across the faces through which the ray leaves, then among all cells that share a vertex with the current cell, and then among the cells that share a vertex with those. A ray that leaves a cell exactly through an edge or vertex, or that only touches a cell, is therefore continued in the cell it enters. Each step advances t, and the lengths of a completed traversal sum to the segment length exactly.

The tracer works on the local cells of a mesh and treats a face whose neighbor is not local as a boundary face.

explicit MeshSegmentTracer(const Mesh &grid, bool split_all_cells = false)

Builds the planes of every local cell and the vertex-to-cell adjacency.

O(cells). Throws std::invalid_argument if the centroid of a non-convex cell lies on the outer side of one of its side triangles.

With split_all_cells, every cell is traced through its side tetrahedra, as a non-convex cell is. The result is the same up to round-off; the option exists to test that path on meshes of convex cells.

bool Trace(std::uint32_t start_cell, const Vector3 &start, const Vector3 &end, std::vector<Segment> &segments) const

Traces the segment from start to end, beginning in the local cell start_cell, which must contain start or have it on its boundary.

Appends the cells that the segment crosses, in order, with the length of the segment in each, to segments.

Returns true when the segment reaches end. Returns false when the segment leaves the local cells first, through a boundary face, a face whose neighbor is not local, or a gap in a non-conforming mesh; segments then holds the part traced before the segment left. Throws if start does not lie in or on start_cell.

std::optional<std::uint32_t> LocateAhead(std::uint32_t hint_cell, const Vector3 &point, const Vector3 &omega) const

Returns the local cell that contains the points just after point along the unit direction omega: hint_cell if it does, else one of the cells near it.

Returns std::nullopt if no local cell does, for example when the ray leaves the mesh at point.

inline bool IsConvexCell(std::uint32_t cell_local_id) const

inline size_t NumThinSideTriangles() const

Number of side triangles, among those whose planes the tracer uses, that are thinner than 1e-3 of their cell.

The direction of a plane computed from such a triangle is uncertain by more than the tracing tolerance at the far side of the cell, so a segment that crosses it may have a small part assigned to the neighboring cell. The constructor warns when this is not zero.
